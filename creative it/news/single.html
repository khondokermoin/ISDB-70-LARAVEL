Monsoon floods displace thousands as rivers breach embankments in the north

Relief teams are being deployed to five districts as water levels continue to rise, with forecasters warning of more rainfall through the weekend.

Nadia Rahman
Staff Correspondent · Bangladesh Desk
Published: July 16, 2026, 2:10 PM
Updated: July 16, 2026, 6:42 PM

Heavy monsoon rainfall over the past week has pushed several major rivers past their danger levels, breaching embankments in low-lying districts and forcing thousands of families from their homes. Local administrations in the north say the situation has deteriorated quickly since Tuesday, when the first embankment failures were reported near riverside settlements.

Relief officials estimate that more than forty thousand people have been displaced so far, with makeshift shelters set up in schools, community centres, and raised highway sections. Boats have become the only means of transport in several unions, where standing water has submerged roads and cut off access to markets and clinics.

"We have never seen the water rise this fast in living memory," said a farmer in Kurigram, describing how his family relocated to a nearby embankment within hours of the breach.

The Directorate of Disaster Management has activated its emergency response protocol, deploying rescue boats and mobile medical units to the worst-hit unions. Officials say priority is being given to elderly residents, pregnant women, and families with young children.

What's driving the rise

Meteorologists point to an unusually active monsoon trough that has stalled over the region, combined with heavy upstream rainfall across the river basin. The Flood Forecasting and Warning Centre says several major rivers are expected to remain above danger levels for at least another 72 hours before a gradual recession begins.

Agriculture officials warn that standing floodwater could damage a significant portion of the region's aman rice seedlings, raising concerns about food security in the coming months. Early estimates suggest tens of thousands of hectares of farmland have already been affected.

Government response

The Prime Minister's office has announced an initial emergency relief allocation to support temporary shelters, clean drinking water, and medical camps in the affected districts. Local MPs have been asked to coordinate distribution with district administrations to avoid duplication of aid efforts.

Aid agencies are calling for sustained support as the situation develops, cautioning that the full scale of damage to homes, livestock, and infrastructure will only become clear once water levels recede.
